Evolutionary Modelling Group 🦕

PI: Piet van den Berg

We develop evolutionary models to understand how living organisms adapt. Using computer simulations and mathematical techniques, we investigate everything from antibiotic resistance to the evolution of human language.

About Our Research

We develop evolutionary models to address fundamental questions about how living organisms adapt to their (often changing) surroundings. To do this, we mainly develop computational models to run evolutionary simulations (in Python and C++), and also use mathematical frameworks to study evolution. In developing our models, we always try to balance simplicity and biological realism – not always an easy task!

Much of our work aims to achieve a general understanding of evolutionary processes in complex situations: how traits evolve under opposing selection pressures, how organisms balance the need for stability with the capacity for change, and how populations adapt to fluctuating environments.

Although some of our work aims for general insight rather than direct predictions, we also collaborate closely with empirical labs to generate specific predictions that can be tested experimentally.

We work on many different topics, but the most important research lines currently include the following:

Antibiotic Resistance

How do bacterial populations evolve resistance? We model the interplay between persistence (where bacteria temporarily become dormant to survive stress) and resistance evolution.

Evolvability & Plasticity

How do organisms evolve the capacity to adapt? We study when evolution favors plasticity, and when it favors a genetic organization that maximizes the capacity to generate adaptive mutations (evolvability).

Mutational Robustness

How do organisms tolerate mutations while maintaining the capacity to adapt? We investigate this fundamental trade-off with different kinds of models.

Language

How did human language evolve? We model how structural and semantic capacities co-evolved to yield human compositional language.

Culture

How does culture change? We use evolutionary tools to study cultural change, and how different forms of cultural transmission (social learning) may have evolved.
